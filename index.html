The Shape of
Stories

Vonnegut drew stories as lines: time across the bottom, fortune up the side. This atlas draws 93 of them — 37 measured from the actual texts, 56 read by hand — and asks his question one step further: fortune isn’t the only axis a story can run on.

Part IV — The finding

The canon runs on crossed axes

Folk tales ride one axis. Again and again, the books that last play two lines against each other — knowledge rising while fortune falls, power rising while the soul drains, and sometimes two readings of the same axis (the lexicon vs. the plot, the telling vs. the timeline). The shaded wedge between the lines is the story’s engine. Sorted by how far the scissors open.

Part V — The map

Every book in shape-space

Horizontal: does the story end better or worse than it began? Vertical: is the middle a peak or a pit? The archetypes fall out as regions — holes sink low, Icarus floats high, tragedies drift left, and the strange ones hug the center.

Appendix

Method & sources

Measured lines

37 public-domain books use per-book sentiment time series computed by Reagan, Mitchell, Kiley, Danforth & Dodds for “The emotional arcs of stories are dominated by six basic shapes” (EPJ Data Science, 2016) — 100 sliding windows per book, scored with the labMT happiness lexicon, published in the andyreagan/core-stories repository. Series are smoothed and normalized per book (Vonnegut drew relative shape, not absolute happiness). Orientation was independently verified by re-deriving five books from their Gutenberg texts with labMT (all correlate positively, r = +0.15 to +0.67).

A sentiment lexicon hears vocabulary, not plot: Wilde narrating atrocity in gorgeous prose reads “happy.” That gap between the lexical line and the felt story is genuine information — not an error.

Authored lines

56 post-1923 and non-novel works (plus every non-fortune axis — no such dataset exists anywhere) are hand-scored editorial readings against a shared calibration rubric: −1 is the worst state canon reaches, 0 is ordinary life, +1 is fairy-tale peak; points sit at actual plot events; endings are encoded honestly (damped recoveries stay damped). They are readings, not measurements — exactly what Vonnegut was doing at the board.

The axes

Beginning→End is constant. The y-axis is a choice: fortune (Vonnegut), knowledge (every mystery), power (the antihero axis), connection (isolation↔belonging), tension (the reader’s axis — cf. Boyd, Blackburn & Pennebaker’s “narrative arc” dimensions, Science Advances 2020), and certainty (Vonnegut’s own Hamlet point).
